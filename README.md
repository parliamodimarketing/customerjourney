# Journey Builder

A tool from *Retail & Digital Marketing — from point of sale to point of experience* (Master Unibo Buenos Aires · Argentina 2026).

**Trace a purchase. Add up every touchpoint. That's the brand.**

Three steps:
1. **The purchase** — what was bought, who bought it, and *the experience we design*: three words for who the brand wants to be.
2. **The journey** — six stops (Trigger · Search · Compare · Buy · Receive & use · After). For every touchpoint you note whether it was **physical or digital**, **who owned it**, and **how it felt**:
   - **We** · owned → design them
   - **Partners** · paid / partner → choose them well
   - **The customer** → make it easy
   - **Everyone else** · earned → influence them
3. **What it says** — the experience curve with its peak and end, the physical/digital mix, who owns the journey, where to act, and *the experience we design* next to *the experience they live*.

There's a worked example (running sneakers) to start from. It works in any browser, with no account and no server. Your journey stays in your browser, and you can copy a summary, print it, or save it as a file and open it again later.

Frameworks: Lemon & Verhoef (2016), *Understanding Customer Experience Throughout the Customer Journey*, Journal of Marketing · owned / paid / earned media · the peak-end rule (Kahneman et al., 1993).

## Publish on GitHub Pages
1. Create a public repository (e.g. `journey-builder`) and upload `index.html` and this `README.md`.
2. Go to Settings → Pages → Source: *Deploy from a branch* → Branch: `main` / root → Save.
3. After a minute the tool is live at `https://<your-username>.github.io/journey-builder/`.
